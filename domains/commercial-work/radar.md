# Radar records

Radar is the family of records through which an observation becomes a reviewed finding or a proposed direction, and through which the organisation keeps what followed. A Radar run starts by command, by a schedule coming due or by a declared reaction to a committed occurrence, the same three ways as any transition. A Radar run proposes a direction or a finding; the record connects that proposal with its observation, evidence, candidate interpretation, review, disposition, follow-up and outcome. These are revisioned domain records with their own sources and relationships. A Radar record is neither a task nor a sales opportunity. The family uses fresh native data; it selects no historical import, research schedule or successor writer. The family hard-codes no research area, department or review topology; an organisation's areas of interest, owners and thresholds are its own configuration.

## A finding cannot accept itself

The writer proposing a finding or a direction cannot move it into accepted state. Review requires the permitted decision-maker, criteria, evidence and policy version, protected by ordinary read and revision checks. A ruling identifies exactly which findings it accepts, rejects, splits, defers or leaves alone. A scheduled result, review reply or planning note is not a ruling. Corrections supersede an earlier ruling rather than rewriting what happened, subject to retention and erasure rules.

A completed run that finds no material change is still a result. Completed means every required check closed and the evidence stayed below the policy's materiality threshold. It records what was checked, the strongest counter-case and what to examine next, so later research can build on it. Missing required evidence instead produces a blocked result: it cannot be presented as a completed search that found nothing. An owner's decision to leave things unchanged is a third, separate event.

An optional Pale integration may coordinate investigation and obtain context, judgment or evaluation from separately selected services. Pale keeps no second Radar store. Context is not an accepted finding; coordinating an operation is not an outside effect, verified outcome or accepted publication. An approved draft, an orchestration operation and a publication release retain separate identities and owners.

## A disposition does not perform the follow-up

A disposition requesting action names the owner permitted to act. That owner independently accepts and commits its work; the Radar record keeps the reference and the later verified outcome. A finding becomes work only when someone takes responsibility for acting on it, with an owner and an acceptance condition; whether that happens depends on the journey that brought the finding in and on the rules the organisation's configuration or the domain sets. No observation, finding or ruling automatically creates work or publication. Direct work creation without a Radar record remains valid.

A possible authorisation-intake operation remains a proposal. It would bind one exact authorisation reference and apply normal work-creation checks: scope, parent, kind of work, assignment permission, budget and expected revision. It would need duplicate detection, commit and read-back, replay, partial-failure and recovery cases. Disabling it must create no duplicate or synthetic work. A Radar record marked applied or assigned would still not be a work item.

## A direction can be tried before anyone decides

A proposal can be a direction as well as a finding: a change the organisation might make, not only an observation about the world. Before the permitted decision-maker rules on it, a proposed direction can be tried by a simulation, such as a projection of what it does to an owner's accounts or a rehearsal of how an audience might react. Weave never runs a simulation. The tool that owns such runs keeps them; the proposal references each run as evidence, with the run's identity, owner, observation date and limits, exactly as [borrowed evidence](#borrowed-evidence-does-not-transfer-a-conclusion) keeps its original reference. A referenced run informs the ruling and is not the ruling, and the decision it informs, whether to spend, publish or change something, belongs to the owner the disposition names.

Once the direction has been acted on and its result is known, the outcome is recorded beside what the proposal expected. Keeping the two together is what makes the record material for later evaluation: a later reader, or an evaluation service reached through Pale, can compare expectation with outcome without reconstructing either. Weave keeps that comparison available, draws no conclusion from it and changes no rule on its own.

## Failure preserves what was actually established

Evidence already gathered can survive a blocked run. When a required source revision or selector is unavailable, or changes during the run without being reconciled, preserve the evidence and label staged inference provisional. Record the missing prerequisite and next safe step; emit no current candidate or handoff from an unresolved run. A change outside the declared dependencies need not invalidate a verified snapshot.

A ruling, its application by the owner and a derived reader view have separate outcomes. Failed validation leaves the prior owner state in place and records the failed attempt. If a view fails after the owner commits, the commit stands and the view reports stale or unavailable state. Read each outcome back separately, and record which of the owner's commit and each view succeeded and which needs repair.

Compensation is a separately permitted transaction with its own result. A state-changing retry needs current permission and preconditions and references the earlier attempt. Exact replay returns the original result without another change. Downstream effects still require the destination's own commit and read-back.

## Borrowed evidence does not transfer a conclusion

An exchange can offer a research dimension without supplying the recipient's decision. The recipient can reject it or find no material change without changing the source owner's conclusion. Any implemented exchange needs its own contract and independently owned acceptance.

When a review uses evaluation results, retain each original reference and its limits. A comparison across evaluator versions is a separate object. A ruling can rely on its conclusion only once the comparison matches the review question and has been accepted for current use. A repeated score label cannot establish comparability after rubric or case coverage changes.

## A future import or writer replacement needs its own proof

Ordinary record imports must not quietly introduce research inboxes, ruling logs, run registers, candidate ledgers or ruling receipts as a new governance system. A separately authorised historical import or writer replacement must preserve meanings and dispositions, identify the replaced writer, exclude parallel writers, handle duplicates and the final delta, and prove independent read-back, recovery and rollback. This does not require reproducing old identifiers or operating machinery. An interface, plugin or generated view cannot silently become a writer or redirect historical results.

The [planned Radar tests](tests/README.md) exercise blocked runs, no-change results, failed application, compensation, retry and downstream effects through this domain.
